The property

A large early Colonial, comprehensively rebuilt

Twenty Harnden Street is one of the larger early houses still standing in Wilmington, and for most of the last century it has been known locally as The Elms. It sits on roughly eight-tenths of an acre a short walk from Silver Lake, with eight parking spaces, public water and public sewer.

The house has come through a full rehabilitation: electrical, plumbing, heating and hot water renewed, Carrier equipment installed across multiple heating and cooling zones, chimneys rebuilt, roughly fifty-eight windows replaced, the exterior stripped and refinished, and the interior taken back to finished condition throughout.

Inside there are hardwood floors, ten fireplaces, and a covered porch off the main rooms. The kitchen is new, built around Bertazzoni appliances with a six-burner double-oven range, dual sinks and extensive cabinetry. A rear patio and mature landscaping complete the site. A walk-up attic offers further space; the basement is unfinished and excluded from the stated living area.

The result is a house that reads as its age from the street and as a current house once you are inside it — which is the trade that makes a building of this size liveable rather than a project.

The Elms

Two centuries on Harnden Street

The house carries the name of the Harnden family and, for a long stretch of the twentieth century, the name The Elms. A fuller account is being researched and will be published here.

The listing records a construction date of 1795, which would place the house among the oldest still standing in Wilmington. That date is one of the things currently being checked against the documentary record rather than repeated as settled fact — town, state and family sources do not all agree, and the reconciliation is part of the work.

What is not in question is that this was a substantial house on a substantial property, that it passed through several notable households, and that by the time of its most recent sale it had declined a long way from that. The rehabilitation that followed was extensive, and it involved real choices — a modern window package rather than custom reproductions, and a red exterior with black trim — that are worth discussing openly rather than smoothing over.

A long-form feature, Saving The Elms, is in preparation and will cover the construction and ownership chronology, the Harnden family, the boarding-house era, the decline, and the decision to rehabilitate rather than replace. It will be sourced and it will distinguish between what the record proves and what is local tradition.

Wilmington

Silver Lake and the town around it

Wilmington sits roughly twenty miles north of Boston, at the meeting of two commuter rail lines and two interstates.

Silver Lake

The house stands a short walk from Silver Lake, the pond the surrounding neighbourhood grew up around and still the centre of its summer life.

Getting to Boston

Wilmington is served by the MBTA Lowell line, with North Station reachable by train, and sits close to Interstates 93 and 95.

The town

A town of roughly twenty-three thousand with its own schools, library and town museum, and an unusually active historical commission.

Commute times, school assignment and district information change and are not warranted here. Buyers should confirm current schedules with the MBTA and current assignment with Wilmington Public Schools.
